7 Compliance Mistakes That Could Cost Barbados Rental Owners Thousands

August 2026·7 min read·INtouch Property Management
‹  All articles

After managing rentals across Barbados, we see the same compliance gaps again and again — usually from owners who are diligent people, just busy. Here are the seven most expensive mistakes, and how to close each one this week.

1. Not registering with the BTPA

The risk: Registration for short-term rentals is open and expected — and under the proposed Tourist Accommodation Bill, operating unlicensed would carry a fine of up to BDS $250,000.
The fix: Register online at barbadostouristaccommodation.com. It's the single highest-value hour you can spend on your rental business.

2. Ignoring the levies

The risk: Villa rentals attract the 10% Shared Economy Levy; vacation rental properties pay the Room Rate Levy (3.75%, capped at US$17.50 per bedroom per night). The 2026 Tourism Levy amendments added fines of $5,000–$10,000 for collection and record-keeping failures.
The fix: Register with the Barbados Revenue Authority, build the levy into your advertised pricing, and file on schedule — every month, no exceptions.

3. Renting without public liability insurance

The risk: One guest injury without cover can erase years of rental income. Insurance documentation is also a required part of the proposed licensing regime.
The fix: Get short-term-rental-specific public liability cover — a standard homeowner policy usually won't respond to paying-guest claims.

4. No fire or health certification

The risk: Both certificates are on the proposed licence application checklist, and both take time to obtain — owners who wait until a deadline will join a very long queue.
The fix: Book the inspections now, fix what they flag, and file the certificates somewhere you can find them.

5. Sloppy (or no) guest records

The risk: The proposed rules require guest registers — name, address, nationality, ID, dates — kept for seven years, plus monthly occupancy reports. You cannot recreate records you never kept.
The fix: Start a simple digital register today. Every booking, every guest, every time.

6. Advertised price ≠ invoice price

The risk: Charging more than the advertised rate, or issuing vague invoices, is a $5,000 offence under the proposed standards — and a guaranteed bad review under today's.
The fix: Itemize everything: nightly rate, cleaning, levies, taxes. Guests trust hosts whose numbers add up.

7. No hurricane / multi-hazard plan

The risk: Beyond the proposed requirement, an unprepared property in storm season is a liability with a roof. Guests booked during a hurricane watch will judge you on your plan.
The fix: A written plan: shutter procedures, emergency contacts, evacuation info, staff responsibilities, supplies. Two pages that could save your business.

The honest truth

None of these fixes is difficult. What sinks owners is doing all seven, on time, every month, while also answering guest messages at midnight and coordinating cleaners. That's precisely the job a professional manager exists to do.
